## Navigating Private School Options for Students with Disabilities in Texas: Understanding the (Proposed) TEFA Program and Parental Due Diligence

For parents of students with disabilities in Texas, exploring private school options can be a complex journey. The prospect of state-funded scholarships, such as the proposed "Texas Education for All" (TEFA) program, often raises questions about accessibility, services, and accountability.

**Important Note:** As of my last update (early 2024), a comprehensive statewide "Texas Education for All" (TEFA) program, often discussed as an Education Savings Account (ESA) or school choice initiative, **has not passed into law** in Texas. While legislative efforts have been made, a statewide program under this specific name or structure is not currently implemented.

However, the questions parents ask about such programs are vital and reflect universal concerns when considering private education for a child with an Individualized Education Program (IEP). This guide will address these questions based on how similar programs typically operate and general best practices for evaluating private schools, should a program like TEFA be implemented in the future.

---

### 1. What a Private School Must Do to Become TEFA-Approved (Hypothetically)

If a program like TEFA were to be implemented, private schools would likely need to meet specific criteria to be eligible to receive state funds. Based on similar ESA programs in other states and legislative proposals, these requirements would typically include:

*   **Accreditation:** The school would likely need to be accredited by a state-recognized accrediting agency or meet specific state educational standards.
*   **Non-Discrimination:** A requirement to adhere to non-discrimination policies, accepting students regardless of race, color, national origin, or religion. While some religious exemptions might exist, broad discrimination based on disability status would likely be prohibited for program participation.
*   **Financial Stability:** Demonstrating financial solvency and responsible management.
*   **Curriculum and Instruction:** Providing a curriculum that meets general educational standards.
*   **Health and Safety:** Compliance with state and local health and safety codes.
*   **Reporting Requirements:** Agreeing to provide regular reports to the state or the scholarship-administering organization regarding student enrollment, attendance, and use of funds.
*   **Parental Choice Agreement:** Signing an agreement to accept scholarship funds as payment for tuition and approved educational expenses.

---

### 2. Do TEFA-Approved Private Schools Have to Provide Special Education Services or Just Accept TEFA Funds?

This is a critical distinction. In the United States, private schools are generally **not bound by the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA)** in the same way public schools are.

*   **Accepting Funds:** If a program like TEFA were in place, approved private schools would accept the scholarship funds as payment for tuition and other approved expenses.
*   **Providing Services:** However, accepting these funds **does not automatically obligate** a private school to provide specific special education services, therapies, or modifications outlined in a public school IEP. Private schools operate independently and determine their own programs and services.
    *   Some private schools *choose* to offer robust special education programs, learning support, or accommodations.
    *   Others may have limited resources or a philosophy that does not align with extensive special education provisions.
    *   Parents would need to inquire directly about the school's capacity and willingness to support their child's specific needs.

---

### 3. How Parents Can Verify a School's Approval Status (If TEFA Were Active)

If a TEFA-like program were active, the process for verifying a school's approval status would typically involve:

*   **Official State Website:** The Texas Education Agency (TEA) or another designated state agency would likely maintain an official, publicly accessible list of approved schools participating in the program.
*   **Scholarship Granting Organizations (SGOs):** Many ESA or scholarship programs are administered by non-profit SGOs. These organizations would also publish lists of eligible schools.
*   **Direct Inquiry:** Parents could directly ask the private school if they are an approved participant in the TEFA program (or any other relevant scholarship program).

---

### 4. What Parents Should Ask During Private School Visits When Their Child Has an IEP

When visiting private schools, especially with a child who has an IEP, parents must be proactive and ask detailed questions. Your child's IEP serves as a valuable guide for these discussions.

**Key Questions to Ask:**

*   **IEP Review and Understanding:**
    *   "How do you review and incorporate information from my child's IEP?"
    *   "Do you have staff trained to interpret IEPs and understand specific disability needs?"
*   **Special Education Staff & Resources:**
    *   "Do you have dedicated learning specialists, special education teachers, or support staff on site?"
    *   "What are their qualifications and experience working with students with [specific disability, e.g., dyslexia, ADHD, autism]?"
    *   "What is the student-to-support staff ratio?"
*   **Accommodations and Modifications:**
    *   "What types of accommodations (e.g., extended time, preferential seating, reduced distractions) can you provide?"
    *   "Are you able to make modifications to the curriculum or assignments if needed?"
    *   "How are accommodations communicated to and implemented by classroom teachers?"
*   **Curriculum and Instruction:**
    *   "How do you differentiate instruction to meet diverse learning needs?"
    *   "What teaching methodologies do you use that might benefit my child?"
    *   "Do you offer any specialized programs (e.g., Orton-Gillingham for dyslexia, executive function coaching)?"
*   **Behavioral and Social-Emotional Support:**
    *   "What behavioral support systems are in place?"
    *   "How do you address social-emotional learning and peer interactions for students with disabilities?"
    *   "Do you have counselors or therapists available?"
*   **Teacher Training:**
    *   "What training do your general education teachers receive regarding special education and inclusive practices?"
*   **Communication and Progress Monitoring:**
    *   "How often will I receive updates on my child's academic and social progress?"
    *   "What is the process for addressing concerns or requesting additional support?"
    *   "How do you track progress on goals (even if not formal IEP goals)?"
*   **Inclusion and Peer Interaction:**
    *   "How are students with disabilities included in general education classes, extracurricular activities, and social events?"
    *   "What is the school's philosophy on inclusion?"
*   **Additional Costs:**
    *   "Are there any additional fees for special education services, tutoring, or therapies that are not covered by tuition or a potential scholarship?"

---

### 5. Do Private Schools Under TEFA Have to Honor the Child's IEP Goals?

No, private schools participating in a scholarship program like TEFA are generally **not legally required to honor or implement a child's IEP goals** in the same way a public school is.

*   **IEP as a Guide:** While the IEP provides valuable information about a child's needs and recommended supports, it serves as a *guide* for discussion with private schools, not a mandate.
*   **School's Discretion:** The private school will determine what services, accommodations, and modifications it is willing and able to provide based on its own resources, philosophy, and programs.
*   **Parental Responsibility:** It is the parent's responsibility to ensure the private school they choose can adequately meet their child's needs, even if it means paying for additional services out-of-pocket or accepting that some IEP goals may not be fully addressed.

---

### 6. What Happens to TEFA Funding If a Private School Placement Doesn't Work Out?

If a private school placement funded by a program like TEFA doesn't work out, the handling of funds would depend on the specific rules of the program and the scholarship-administering organization. Generally, you can expect:

*   **Prorated Funds:** Scholarship funds are typically disbursed for a specific enrollment period (e.g., per semester or per year). If a student withdraws mid-period, the remaining funds would likely be prorated.
*   **Return to Public School:** If a child returns to a public school, the scholarship funds would cease, and the public school would resume its obligation under IDEA to provide a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) and implement the child's IEP.
*   **Transfer to Another Approved School:** If the family wishes to transfer to another TEFA-approved private school, the remaining funds might be transferable, subject to the program's rules and the new school's acceptance.
*   **Unused Funds:** Any unused funds might revert to the scholarship program or be subject to specific refund policies. It's crucial to understand the withdrawal and refund policies of both the scholarship program and the private school before enrollment.

---

### Conclusion: Empowering Parents Through Diligence

While a statewide "Texas Education for All" (TEFA) program is not currently active, the questions it raises are fundamental for parents considering private school options for students with disabilities. The key takeaway is **parental due diligence**.

Private schools offer diverse environments, and some are exceptionally well-equipped to support students with special needs, while others may not be. Thorough research, direct communication, and careful evaluation of a school's capacity to meet your child's unique requirements are paramount. Always verify a school's offerings, understand its limitations, and clarify all financial implications, especially concerning specialized services, to ensure the best possible educational fit for your child. Stay informed about legislative developments regarding school choice in Texas, as programs can evolve.
